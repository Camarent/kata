TCP is a connection-oriented transport protocol. It sets up a connection with a handshake, numbers every segment, and resends anything lost, so the receiver gets the bytes in order and without gaps. The cost is extra round trips and some delay when packets are lost. UDP skips the handshake and the resends. It sends independent datagrams with less overhead, which suits live audio, video and games, where a late packet is worse than a lost one. Applications that need reliable delivery over UDP must add their own checks.
